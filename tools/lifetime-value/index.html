How to read it Contribution, not revenue — and payback, not just value

Everything here runs on contribution: what actually reaches you after the platform's cut, not the headline price. A customer's lifetime value is that contribution multiplied by how many times they buy over their life with you. But value alone can bankrupt you if it arrives too slowly — which is why payback is the number that governs how fast you can grow.

The formulas

Contribution per order = average order value × (1 − fee%). Lifetime value = contribution per order × lifetime orders. Payback is the book's definition: the point at which a customer's cumulative contribution covers what they cost to acquire. If your first-order contribution already exceeds the acquisition cost, they are recouped in the first order — the strongest position there is. Otherwise it comes on a later order — the result tells you which one.

A worked example

$44 average order at 6.5% keeps $41.14 of contribution. If a customer buys 1.45 times over their life, their lifetime value is $41.14 × 1.45 = $59.65. If they cost $28.80 to acquire, that is less than the $41.14 they contribute on the very first order — so they are recouped in the first order, and spending up to your ceiling is covered by the first order alone beyond it.

Edge cases the calculator handles

  • A customer who never repays — if even their whole lifetime value stays below what they cost to acquire, payback reads "does not pay back over their life" rather than a made-up month count. That is the signal to fix the offer or lower the acquisition cost.
  • A fee of 100% or more leaves no contribution, so every line shows "—": there is nothing to have a lifetime value of.

Common questions

Why must lifetime orders be at least 1?

Because every acquired customer has, by definition, bought at least once — that is what made them a customer. A figure below 1 is not possible, so the calculator holds the floor at 1.0 (which represents a customer who never comes back).

Should I set my ad budget from lifetime value?

Careful — no. Paying up to lifetime value assumes a future that has not happened yet. Spend against first-order contribution until a real paid cohort proves its own payback. The CAC ceiling calculator works out the safe number.
