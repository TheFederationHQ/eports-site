Test a print-on-demand shop: five holiday products and what each costs you

A print-on-demand shop lets you sell mugs, tees and ornaments with your designs on them without buying stock or owning a printer. Here are five holiday products, what Printful charges you to make and ship each one, what you keep after Etsy's fees, and a one-week plan to test them before Christmas.

Edited by Drew Halden19 Nov 2026 Sources checked 19 Nov 2026

ShareXFacebookLinkedIn

The short version: A custom mug costs you $13.16 to have Printful make and ship to a US buyer this season. Price it at $22 on Etsy and you keep about $6.30 after Etsy's fees, with no stock to buy and nothing to pack. That's the whole idea of print-on-demand: you make the design, a printer makes each item only after someone orders it. With Black Friday on November 27 and Christmas five weeks away, this is the busiest time of year to test whether your designs sell.

Who this is for: anyone in the US who can make or commission a design and wants to try selling it without spending money on inventory first.

How print-on-demand works

You upload a design to a print company such as Printful and connect it to a shop, such as Etsy. When a buyer orders, the order passes to Printful, which prints the item, packs it and ships it under your shop's name. Printful charges you for the product and the shipping. Etsy takes its fees from the sale. What's left is yours.

There's no stock sitting in your house, so a design that doesn't sell costs you nothing but the time to make it and a $0.20 Etsy listing fee.

The turn: shipping and fees decide which products are worth it

Here's what most beginners miss. The product price looks cheap, but you also pay shipping on every order, and Etsy charges its fees on the full price the buyer pays, shipping included. On a US Etsy shop, every sale costs:

  • $0.20 to list the item (charged again each time the listing renews).
  • 6.5% transaction fee on the item price plus any shipping you charge.
  • 3% plus $0.25 payment processing, on the whole order including tax.

These are our example prices, not what the market will pay. Fees here are before sales tax, which adds a little to the processing fee. The lesson is in the hoodie rows: price it like a tee and you keep under $4. Price the expensive products with room to spare, or don't stock them at all.

If Etsy's offsite ads bring you a sale, Etsy takes a further 15% of that order (12% once your shop passes $10,000 a year in sales). Price with that in mind.

Pick one design idea, put it on all five

You don't know yet which product your buyers want, and that's what the test is for. Make one strong holiday design, such as a family name, a pet, a hometown or a hobby joke, and put it on all five products. Then watch which listing gets the views, the favorites and the sales.

Personalized items, where the buyer adds a name, tend to suit gifts. Keep the design your own. Using someone else's characters, logos or art can get your listing taken down.

Two Etsy rules to follow from day one

  • Say who makes it. Etsy's Creativity Standards say you must disclose that an item is made by a production partner. In your listing, add Printful as your production partner.
  • Set honest delivery times. Printful says apparel takes longer to make than other products, and it publishes its holiday order cut-off dates product by product on its holiday deadlines page. Check them before you promise Christmas delivery.

Do this now

  1. Make one design today, and keep it simple enough to read on a small ornament.
  2. Open a free Printful account and connect your Etsy shop. Add the design to all five products.
  3. Price each product with our free print-on-demand profit calculator before you list it. Leave room for a holiday discount.
  4. Order one sample yourself of the product you expect to sell most, so your photos and description match what buyers get.
  5. Check your stats after a week. Keep the products that get views and favorites, and drop the rest.

What's still unknown: whether your designs will sell. Many first listings get few views, and a test with five products costs you about $1 in Etsy listing fees and the time to make one design. For more on Etsy's fees, see our Etsy fee calculator.
