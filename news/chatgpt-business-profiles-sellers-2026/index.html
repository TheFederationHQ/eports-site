ChatGPT now lists businesses: how to set up your shop's profile, and its limits

ChatGPT now lets a business claim and edit how it shows up, and OpenAI lists no fee to claim. Here's how to set up your shop's profile, who qualifies, what Shopify and Etsy sellers already get, the free data worth having, and what it won't do for your sales.

Edited by Drew Halden16 Nov 2026 Sources checked 15 Nov 2026

ShareXFacebookLinkedIn

The short version: If you sell from your own website, you can now claim your shop's profile in ChatGPT and choose what it shows people: your description, hours, products and links. OpenAI lists no fee to claim, in a beta that opened on November 10. But claiming doesn't guarantee higher placement or more sales, OpenAI says, and its terms ask a lot of you. The real value is getting your details right and free data on the topics you come up for. Don't pay an agency to do it for you.

Who can ignore this: Etsy-only sellers with no website of their own. The profile is built around a business website, and Etsy already handles how its listings show up in ChatGPT.

What OpenAI launched

Businesses in ChatGPT lets a business claim its profile in a Business Hub, prove it owns the business, and then edit the details ChatGPT shows, such as its description, website, hours, products and links.

You start the claim with a business phone number, a business email, your website's domain or your Shopify store. OpenAI reviews it and emails its decision, so it isn't instant. Then one person passes an identity check run by Persona, an ID company, which can ask for a government ID, business papers and proof you're allowed to act for the business.

You can't publish changes, add a product feed or see your data until that check is approved. The blue check on your profile appears only after it.

Who can use it

Eligible businesses can claim where ChatGPT is available. OpenAI decides by industry, location and its own checks, and its product page says the offer starts with "eligible businesses with a website". Local shops and restaurants can only claim an existing US listing for now.

A second tool, Business Agents, answers questions about your products and policies inside ChatGPT and links to your site. It's US-only, for eligible online stores and service companies with a website. It can't take orders, make bookings or look up an order.

The turn: a claim is a correction tool, not an ad

This is the part the "claim it now" videos leave out. OpenAI's help page says claiming doesn't change your visibility and doesn't guarantee higher placement or more sales. You get no control over ChatGPT's own recommendations, and OpenAI says ads don't change its independent answers either.

What a claim does give you is a way to correct your hours, links, prices and description. That's worth an hour of your own time. It's not something you need to pay a setup fee for.

Paid placement is separate. A business can sponsor its agent as a labeled ad. OpenAI is also offering new US advertisers $500 in ad credit when they spend $500 on ChatGPT ads by December 31, and credits usually expire 90 days after they're issued. That's an ad offer, not a listing fee, and you don't need it to claim a profile.

The free data is the real prize

Once you're verified, you see the last 28 days: how often ChatGPT mentions you, clicks to your website, and the topics you come up for, which OpenAI calls themes in answers that cited you. You never see anyone's chats or exact questions.

Two catches. For online shops, a mention only counts when ChatGPT points to your website. Your brand name alone doesn't count. And numbers only show above a minimum, so a small shop may see blanks. OpenAI says a blank doesn't always mean zero.

Even so, the topics list is a free source of keyword ideas. If "gifts for a new nurse" keeps showing up as a theme when your shop comes up, that's a phrase for your titles, tags and next product.

A note on fees, since this is where rumors spread. In January, reports said Shopify stores would pay a 4% fee on sales through ChatGPT's Instant Checkout. OpenAI scaled Instant Checkout back in March, and Shopify's help page now says there are no fees for selling in ChatGPT beyond standard payment processing.

Selling on your own site, not Shopify? OpenAI accepts a product feed: a CSV, TSV or JSONL file, or a link to one, with nine required fields such as title, price, link and stock. It's US-only for now. Books can use a 13-digit ISBN as the product code.

Before you claim: what the terms say

Publishing a profile means accepting OpenAI's Businesses in ChatGPT Terms. In plain words:

  • You cover OpenAI's legal costs if someone sues over your data, products, listing or ads, or says your claims were misleading.
  • You promise not to sue OpenAI over the data you submit or content on your own websites, except if OpenAI breaks these terms.
  • Your basic details stay licensed to OpenAI for good: your name, address, contacts, category, hours and website. OpenAI can show a listing for you whether you claim it or not.
  • A product feed opens your site to OpenAI's crawlers, and any "no AI training or mining" opt-out you've set doesn't apply to OpenAI.
  • You can't easily take it back. Only draft profiles can be deleted, and a new profile can go live as soon as the ID check is approved.
  • ChatGPT may phone your business for its users unless you opt out.

The ID check also ties a real person's name to the account, which matters if you sell under a pen name. Read the terms yourself before you start. This isn't legal advice.

What to do this week

  1. Ask ChatGPT about your own shop. Type "What do you know about [your shop name]?" and "Where can I buy [your best product]?" Write down anything wrong.
  2. Fix it at the source first. ChatGPT reads the web. Make your website's About page, prices, shipping and returns current and easy to read. That helps whether you claim or not.
  3. Decide on the terms, then claim it yourself if you're happy. OpenAI's setup guide links to the Business Hub. OpenAI lists no fee. Set aside about an hour, plus review time.
  4. Shopify sellers: in your Shopify admin, open Sales channels and check the ChatGPT (agentic) settings. Make sure your stock counts are right.
  5. Check your insights after 28 days, and copy the topics into your listing notes. Selling on Etsy too? Our free Etsy fee calculator shows what each sale really keeps.
