What the dates mean

How to read this table

Last spring frost is the median date of the last 32°F night, so in half of years the last frost comes later. Gardeners usually wait a week or two after it before planting tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil.

"Frost still likely" is an earlier date: in 9 years out of 10, there's at least one more frost after it. Seeds and transplants that need warmth shouldn't go out before this date.

First fall frost is the median date of the first 32°F night in autumn. "Early frost risk" is the date by which an early freeze has arrived in 1 year out of 10.

Growing season is the median number of days between the last spring frost and the first fall frost.

FAQ

Illinois frost dates, answered

When is the last frost in Illinois?

It depends a lot on where you are. Across 170 NOAA weather stations in Illinois, the typical last spring frost ranges from Apr 2 to May 2, with a middle value of Apr 18. Find the station nearest you in the table.

When is the first frost in Illinois?

The typical first fall frost runs from Oct 7 to Nov 5 across the state, with a middle value of Oct 21.

How accurate are frost dates?

They're 30-year averages (1991 to 2020) from NOAA, so they tell you the odds, not this year's weather. Check your local forecast before planting, and cover tender plants on cold nights.

Source and method

NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, 1991–2020 US Climate Normals (annual/seasonal, by station), 32°F freeze probabilities, downloaded 15 November 2026. "Last spring frost" and "first fall frost" are the 50% dates, "frost still likely before" is the 90% spring date, and "early frost risk from" is the 10% fall date.
