What the dates mean

How to read frost dates

Last spring frost is the median date of the last 32°F night, so in half of years the last frost comes later. Gardeners usually wait a week or two after it before planting tender crops like tomatoes, peppers and basil.

"Frost still likely" is an earlier date: in 9 years out of 10, there's at least one more frost after it. Seeds and transplants that need warmth shouldn't go out before this date.

First fall frost is the median date of the first 32°F night in autumn. "Early frost risk" is the date by which an early freeze has arrived in 1 year out of 10.

Growing season is the median number of days between the last spring frost and the first fall frost.

Get ahead

Beat the frost

Start seeds indoors

Most tomatoes and peppers are sown indoors 6 to 8 weeks before the last frost. A heat mat and a simple grow light make the difference between strong and leggy seedlings.

Cover on cold nights

Frost cloth (row cover) laid over plants holds in ground heat and protects against light frost, in spring and in fall.

FAQ

Frost dates, answered

When is the last frost date where I live?

Enter your ZIP code or tap “Near me”. We find the nearest of NOAA's 6,948 frost-recording weather stations and show its typical last spring frost, first fall frost and growing season.

What does the last frost date mean?

It's the date after which half of years have no more 32°F nights, based on 1991 to 2020. In the other half, a frost still comes later, so many gardeners wait a week or two before planting tender crops.

When should I plant tomatoes?

Tomatoes, peppers, squash and basil are killed by frost and sulk in cold soil. Plant them out one to two weeks after your last frost date, once nights stay above about 50°F.

Which plants can go in before the last frost?

Cool-season crops such as peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes and onions handle light frost and are usually sown two to six weeks before the last frost date.
