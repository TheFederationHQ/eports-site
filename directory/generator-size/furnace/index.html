Stay safe

Two rules that save lives

Never run a generator indoors, in a garage or near a window. Its exhaust carries carbon monoxide, which kills within minutes and has no smell. Keep it at least 20 feet from the house with the exhaust pointing away, and put a battery CO alarm on every floor.

Never plug a generator into a wall outlet. Backfeeding can electrocute line workers and start fires. To power house circuits, have an electrician fit a transfer switch or interlock kit. Otherwise run heavy-duty extension cords to each appliance.

FAQ

Generator sizing, answered

What size generator do I need for a gas furnace?

A gas furnace needs about 750 running watts and 1,875.0 watts for a second as it starts. A 2,500 W inverter generator covers it with headroom.

How do I work out what size generator I need?

Add up the running watts of everything you need on at the same time, add the extra the biggest motor needs for a second as it starts (often two to three times its running watts), then add about 20% headroom. The calculator on this page does it as you tap.

What's the difference between running watts and starting watts?

Running watts are what an appliance draws once it's going. Starting watts are the short surge a motor, like a fridge compressor or a pump, needs to get moving. Generators list both; your starting peak must fit under the generator's starting rating.

Can I plug my generator into a wall outlet?

No. That's called backfeeding, and it can electrocute utility workers and start fires. Use extension cords to each appliance, or have an electrician fit a transfer switch or interlock kit.

Is an inverter generator better?

For electronics and noise, yes: inverter generators make cleaner power and run quieter. Above about 4,000 watts, conventional portable generators cost much less per watt.
