How to read it Four instruments, one decision

Most print-on-demand sellers run on gross revenue and gut feel, and it hides the one thing that matters: which designs actually make money once every cost is counted, and where the next dollar and the next hour should go. These four instruments answer that, in order.

Loaded (fully-burdened) contribution tells you what a design really leaves once ads, returns, overhead and an honest cost for your own time come out — not the flattering sticker margin. It keeps the pre-ad figure separate, because that is the only number a customer-acquisition cost should ever be judged against. The CAC ceiling and break-even ROAS tell you the most you can pay to win a buyer before an ad turns a sale into a loss — and expose why a "3× return on ad spend" the dashboard cheers can still be a bleed on a thin product. The coupon check shows a discount's true cost: because the platform's percentage fee falls with the price, the real hit is the discount times one-minus-the-fee-rate, not the sticker figure. And contribution per owner-hour ranks your catalog by the resource you are actually short of — so a thin-margin, hands-free design can rightly out-rank a fat one that eats your week.

Every figure is illustrative and yours to change — type in your own numbers and the sheet recalculates live, entirely in your browser, nothing stored or sent. It is checked against the same test cases as the free downloadable spreadsheet, so the tool and the workbook agree on every figure they share. Together they turn allocation from a feeling into a decision you can defend — the whole method of Print on Demand — Advanced.
