Drafting with AI took 3 hours. The job took 9. How to price an AI service for a local shop

If you price a monthly AI content package for a local shop by the time the AI saves you, you'll quote about $150 for work that really takes 9 hours. Here's a worked example for a bakery, hour by hour, with tools, card fees and revision limits, that lands at $475 a month before tax.

The short version: If you price an AI content package for a local shop by the 3 hours the AI drafting takes, you'll quote about $150 for a job that really takes 9. Our bakery example comes to $475 a month: 9 hours of work, plus tools and card fees, before your own taxes.

Who can ignore this: anyone who already prices from the time real jobs take, not from a guess. This is for people about to quote their first local client.

The job

Our example is a small bakery that wants a monthly package: 12 social posts, 4 product descriptions for its website, and 1 email newsletter. The owner wants it to sound like them, not like a robot.

This is our own worked example, not survey data. It assumes the bakery supplies its own photos and already has a logo and brand colors. Photography, graphic design, replying to comments and the first month's setup are extra, so price them separately. Swap in your own hours as you go.

The part most new freelancers miss

The AI drafting is quick. Here's where the rest of a month goes:

TaskHours
Drafting with AI3
Editing for the owner's voice, and checking prices, dates and claims3
A monthly call, plus two rounds of changes2
Scheduling, invoicing and admin1
Total9

Only a third of the time is the part people picture when they hear "AI service". The editing and checking is the part the shop is really paying for, because a wrong price or opening time in a post costs them customers.

At $50 an hour, 9 hours is $450. Quote by the drafting time alone and you'd ask for $150, a third of that.

Add the costs you'll carry

Tools. ChatGPT Plus costs $20 a month. If you serve three clients, that's about $7 each. Add your share of any design or scheduling tool you pay for.

Card fees. If the bakery pays a $475 invoice by card through Stripe, the standard US processing rate of 2.9% plus $0.30 takes about $14. Stripe's invoicing and billing products can add their own fees.

Tools and card fees add about $21 to the $450 of time, so we round up to $475 a month.

Then set tax money aside

The $475 covers your time and costs. It isn't what you take home. As a US sole proprietor, once your net earnings from self-employment reach $400 in a year, you'll generally owe self-employment tax of 15.3%, on top of income tax.

It's usually worked out on 92.35% of your net earnings from self-employment, and you may need to pay it in quarterly estimates. Put a share of every invoice into a separate account so the bill doesn't surprise you.

Put limits on revisions

Revisions are where small jobs quietly double. Write the limit into your quote: two rounds of changes a month, with the owner's notes sent together by a set day, and extra rounds billed at your hourly rate.

The same goes for scope. If the bakery asks for a 13th post or a second newsletter, that's an add-on, not a favor.

What to do this week

  1. Time one real job, start to finish, including calls and admin. Use that, not a guess.
  2. Pick your hourly rate and multiply. Our free project price calculator does the sum.
  3. Add your tool share and card fees, then round up. Then decide what share of each invoice goes into your tax account.
  4. Write a one-page agreement: what's included each month, payment before the month's work starts, the revision rounds and approval deadline, how either side can cancel, who owns the finished posts, and the price for extras.
  5. Tell the client you use AI tools, and agree what you'll do with their information. Don't paste in customer lists or anything private without their say-so.

What's still unknown: what shops near you will pay. That varies by town and by trade, so ask two or three owners what they spend on marketing now before you settle on a number.
