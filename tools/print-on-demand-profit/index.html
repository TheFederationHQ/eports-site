How to read it Contribution is the number the business runs on

Contribution is what a sale actually leaves you once the printer, the shipping and the platform have taken their share. It is not profit for the month — there is no rent or ad spend in it — but it is the honest per-sale reward, and every later decision (how many you must sell, what you can spend to find a buyer) runs on it.

⚠️ Charging the buyer for shipping is not free money. Notice what happens if you move £4.80 from the item price into the shipping box: the buyer still pays the same total, and the platform still takes its cut on that total — so your contribution barely moves. What actually changes it is the total the buyer pays, or your costs.

The formula, in full

Contribution = (item price + shipping you charge) − base cost − shipping you pay − refund buffer − fixed fee − platform fee. The platform fee is a percentage of the whole amount the buyer pays you (item + charged shipping), not just the item; the fixed fee is any flat per-sale charge on top of it (Etsy's listing fee, plus the fixed part of payment processing).

A worked example

A £22 tee with "free" shipping: base cost £9, you pay £4.80 shipping, a 10% platform fee, and a £0.50 buffer for the odd refund. The buyer pays £22, so the platform takes £2.20. What reaches you is £22 − £9 − £4.80 − £0.50 − £2.20 = £5.50 — about a quarter of the sticker price. Add a flat fee — Etsy's ~£0.16 listing fee, say — and the same sale nets £5.34. That is the shape of apparel print on demand, and it is why the price has to be set on purpose.

Edge cases the calculator handles

  • Free vs charged shipping at the same total. £22 delivered and £17.20 + £4.80 shipping give the same contribution — because the buyer's total, and the fee on it, are the same.
  • A blank or negative price shows "—" rather than a nonsense figure.
  • Contribution can come out negative — that is a real answer: the product loses money at that price, and the calculator shows it in the red instead of hiding it.

Common questions

What fee percentage should I use?

Your platform's all-in figure, as a percentage. On Etsy that is roughly 10% — the 6.5% transaction fee plus payment processing, charged separately — and nearer 12–13% for a UK seller once VAT on the fees is counted. The current figures, with dates, are on the Print on Demand resources page.

What is a healthy contribution?

Working guidance for a beginner: if what reaches you is under about £4–£5 a sale, stop and rethink — change the price, the product, the base cost or the shipping — before you go further. A thin margin is not always fatal, but it makes everything downstream harder.
