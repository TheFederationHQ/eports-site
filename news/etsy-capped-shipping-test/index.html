Etsy is paying part of some US buyers' shipping. Your earnings and fees stay the same

Etsy is testing a cap on what some US buyers pay for calculated shipping, and covering the gap itself. Etsy has now confirmed your fees are still worked out on the full shipping amount, so nothing changes for you.

Edited by Drew Halden21 Oct 2026 · Updated 23 Oct 2026 Sources checked 23 Oct 2026

ShareXFacebookLinkedIn

Updated October 23, 2026: Etsy has answered the open question in our first version of this story. Fees are still charged on the full shipping amount, so there's no saving, and no loss, for you. We've rewritten the story around that answer.

The short version: Etsy is testing a cap on what some US buyers pay for calculated shipping, and Etsy pays the difference. Your item and shipping earnings stay the same, and so do your fees. You don't need to change anything.

Who can ignore this: shops that charge a fixed shipping price or offer free shipping. The test only touches orders that use calculated shipping.

What Etsy is doing

Etsy has emailed US sellers about a "limited, temporary, randomized" test. On some eligible orders that use calculated shipping, the buyer is charged a lower, capped shipping price, and Etsy covers the gap. The test is expected to run for a few weeks.

Etsy's own example, from the email as reproduced by Value Added Resource: your calculated shipping rate is $10, and the buyer is charged a capped $7.99.

You still receive the full $10. Etsy pays the other $2.01. Caps vary by order; $7.99 is the highest one Etsy is testing.

Your fees don't change either

When the test was first reported, nobody knew whether Etsy's fees would be charged on your full $10 or on the $7.99 the buyer paid. Etsy has now told Value Added Resource that transaction and payment-processing fees are still calculated on the full shipping amount, Etsy's share included.

So an order in the test looks exactly like a normal one from your side: same earnings, same fees. The only difference is that your buyer paid a little less for shipping, and Etsy made up the gap.

How you'll know an order was in the test

The difference shows as "Etsy-funded shipping" in that order's Order Details. Your Payment Account still shows your normal shipping revenue. Etsy says no listing or shipping-setting changes are needed. The reports don't say whether a shop can opt out.

Why Etsy is doing this

Etsy has said before that high shipping prices put buyers off. Since 2024 it has favored eligible US listings with shipping under $6 in search, but listings on calculated shipping were left out of that change, as Value Added Resource reported at the time.

This test looks like Etsy checking whether a cheaper shipping price brings more sales to calculated-shipping shops, with Etsy paying for it rather than you. Etsy says the results may inform future improvements to shipping. It hasn't said what those might be, and that's the part worth watching.

What to do

  1. Nothing to change. Keep your calculated shipping as it is.
  2. Watch your Order Details for "Etsy-funded shipping", so you know if your shop is in the test.
  3. Know your real take-home on a normal order. If shipping is a big part of your price, put one order into the free fee calculator for Etsy sellers. It shows what Etsy leaves you after every fee, shipping included.
