What changed for online sellers in 2026: the worries, the wins, and who it hits

Ten months in, the main fee you pay when something sells has stood still. Postage, parcels to Europe, eBay payouts and Etsy's photo rules are where 2026 moved. Here's what each change means for a small shop, in plain English.

Edited by Drew Halden29 Oct 2026 Sources checked 29 Oct 2026

ShareXFacebookLinkedIn

The short version: If you sell on Etsy, eBay or Mercari, the main fee you pay when something sells is the same today as it was in January. What moved this year is everything around the sale. USPS changed its prices four times, Europe added new charges on parcels, eBay changes how you get paid next week, and Etsy rewrote its photo rules.

None of it is a reason to panic. Most of it is a reason to spend one quiet evening checking your prices.

Who can ignore most of this: US shops that sell only downloads that aren't patterns or recipes. The postage and Europe changes don't touch you. Read your row, then skip to the good news.

The fees held still. The cost of getting a parcel there didn't.

Etsy's transaction fee is still 6.5% and a listing still costs 20 cents. Etsy revised its fee policy on October 5 without changing either one. eBay's and Mercari's main selling fees for US sellers haven't moved either.

So if you've been bracing for a fee hike, it hasn't come. The money went somewhere else: the label.

USPS raised its prices on January 18. On April 26 it added a temporary 8% on its main parcel services, which runs until January 17, 2027.

On July 12 it changed how some business label prices are worked out, including for parcels under a pound and for large, light boxes. Then, from October 4, a holiday increase landed on top, also until January 17.

Here's what that means for one order. Say a label cost you $6.00 last year, and you still charge $6.00 for shipping. The 8% on its own adds about 48 cents, before the January and holiday changes.

Etsy also takes 6.5% of what the buyer pays for shipping, which is 39 cents on $6.00. So that $6.00 charge now leaves you well short of what the label costs, and the gap comes out of your profit on every order.

That's the thing most sellers miss this year. The price list on the marketplace looks the same, so it feels like nothing changed. Your margin changed anyway. Etsy Basics walks through setting a shipping price that covers the label and the fee charged on it.

The worrying side

Four changes cost money, or catch people out if they don't know about them.

Mercari labels and print partners. Mercari's prepaid labels changed on January 20, again on May 7 to add the USPS surcharge, and again for some weights on October 19. Print partners Printful and Printify add 40 cents to each US order from October 15 to January 17.

Parcels to Europe. Since July 1 the EU has charged a €3 customs duty on low-value parcels from outside the EU. From November 1 it adds a €2 handling fee. Both are charged once for each different product in the parcel, not for each copy.

So a parcel with two identical mugs counts as one product: €3 of duty and €2 of handling. A parcel with a pen, a notebook and a key ring counts as three: €9 and €6. The handling fee isn't refunded if the buyer sends the parcel back. If you sell to EU buyers, decide before November 1 whether those charges go into your price or come out of your margin.

eBay payouts. eBay's new balance terms take effect for existing US sellers on November 4. Value Added Resource, which reported eBay's notice to sellers, says payout schedules switch to on-demand once a seller is enrolled. Daily, weekly and monthly payouts are still available. You just have to set yours again after November 4, or your money waits at eBay until you ask for it.

Etsy's photo and AI rules. Since October 22, most Etsy listings need at least one unedited photo of the real item on a real background. Print-on-demand items can use a stock mockup you're allowed to use, and downloads have their own rules. AI-written patterns and recipes are banned. The risk to watch: if a listing misses the rules and a buyer opens a case saying the item is different, Etsy may refund the buyer and you may be the one paying.

The good side

The $600 tax form isn't coming. For years, sellers were told that marketplaces and payment apps would send a Form 1099-K once sales passed $600. A 2025 law moved the line back to more than $20,000 and more than 200 sales in a year. A platform can still send one for less, and some states set lower limits. You owe tax on your profit either way.

Etsy is paying back more of its fee for the holidays. Until January 4, invited sellers in Etsy's Share & Save program get the full 6.5% transaction fee back on eligible sales that come through their own shared links. The usual refund is 4%.

Etsy's buyer protection got bigger. Since May 7, Etsy's Purchase Protection covers up to $250 of the refund on eligible orders of any size. Before, only orders of $250 or less were covered. That's a real help on the day a parcel goes missing.

What to do this week

  1. Reprice shipping against today's postage, not last year's.
  2. Shipping to the EU: decide before November 1 who pays the new charges.
  3. Etsy sellers: add one unedited photo to your best sellers, then work down the list.
  4. eBay sellers: after November 4, open Seller Hub, go to Payments, and set your payout schedule.
  5. In Share & Save? Share your links now, while the refund is the full 6.5%.

What we're still watching

Several seller guides report that TikTok Shop raised its US referral fee from 6% to 8% in August. TikTok's own public fee table still showed 6% when it was last checked, so we haven't counted it here. If you sell there, your settlement statement is the place to look.
