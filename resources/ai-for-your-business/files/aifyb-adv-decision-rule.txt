THE DECISION-RULE CARD
From "AI for Your Business — Advanced", chapter 3
================================================================

A finding in a document has no trigger, no owner and no moment at which a named
person must act. It sits outside the working week, and the working week always
wins. That is why good analysis changes nothing.

This card fixes that for exactly ONE recurring decision. Written down, a
decision improves. In someone's head, it just ages.

⚠️ ONE RULE, ONE QUARTER. You will spot five decisions that need this. Five
rules written at once means five unrevised rules in December, and the revision
is where all the value is.

⚠️ NOTHING HERE IS A BUILD. No wiring, no agent, no workflow. You are writing a
card and four names. Every firm that skipped straight to automating a decision
it never wrote down ended up automating its worst assumptions, at speed, with
nobody able to say why.


0 - CHOOSE THE DECISION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Two tests, and both must pass.

  [ ] DOES IT CROSS A BOUNDARY between two people?
      If the same person spots the situation, decides, and lives with the
      consequence, nothing gets codified -- they just keep doing what they do
      with a document nearby.

  [ ] DOES IT CURRENTLY REQUIRE A SPECIFIC PERSON'S HEAD?
      If yes, that's your one. If anyone could do it, it's a process, not a
      judgment, and it doesn't need a rule.

  [ ] Does it happen at least monthly? (It must, or you'll never accumulate
      enough cases to revise on.)

  The decision: ________________________________________________


THE RULE CARD
================================================================

1  PATTERN IT CAME FROM
   (from your chapter-2 record -- how many judged outcomes, and what they
   cluster on. A rule with no evidence behind it is just a policy.)

   ______________________________________________________________
   ______________________________________________________________

2  TRIGGER
   (observable -- something a person notices without interpreting.
    "A client who seems likely to be confused" is a mood, not a trigger.)

   ______________________________________________________________

3  DEFAULT ACTION
   (what happens unless someone stops it. A default, not a command.)

   ______________________________________________________________

4  WHAT THE AI DOES
   At the moment of the decision it produces three lines for a person to read:

      Trigger matched:  yes / no, and on what
      Recommended:      the default action
      Because:          the past cases from OUR OWN record it is matching

   ⚠️ THE CITATION RULE, in one line: NO CITED CASE FROM OUR OWN RECORD, NO
   RECOMMENDATION. If it can't cite, the flag doesn't appear and the decision
   is made the old way. That single line of policy is what stops a confident-
   sounding machine inventing grounds that were never in your business -- and
   it is a management instruction, not a technical control. Read the third
   line before acting on the second.

   Then it stops. It does not decide, does not send, does not act. If your rule
   can't be run on paper by a person who has read it, it isn't a rule yet.

5  PROPOSER          ______________________________________________
   (spots the trigger, puts up the default -- usually whoever's doing the work)

6  REVIEWER, WHO DECIDES  _________________________________________
   (a DIFFERENT person -- that's the boundary)

7  MAY OVERRIDE ON
   (name the KIND of reason, not just the person. "Client-specific knowledge --
    something true of that client the trigger can't see." NOT "being busy.")

   ______________________________________________________________

   A rule with no override gets quietly ignored within a month.
   A rule with unlimited override was never a rule.

8  EXCEPTION AUTHORITY  ___________________________________________
   (a THIRD person, for cases outside the stated override grounds. If the
    reviewer also owns every exception, "exception" quietly becomes "whatever
    the reviewer prefers.")

9  OUTCOME LOGGER  ________________________________________________
   (often the recorder from chapter 2 -- and it can be. Say so rather than
    assuming.)

10 WHAT GETS LOGGED
   BOTH the followed cases and the overrides, with a reason for the override in
   one sentence. This is the part everyone drops, and dropping it is what stops
   the rule ever getting better.

   ⚠️ OVERRIDES ARE NOT FAILURES. They are the most valuable entries you have
   -- each one is an experienced person telling you exactly where your rule is
   too crude, in the only language that carries weight: a real case.

11 REVISION DATE  _________________________________________________
   (a quarter out, on the calendar now. On that date the overrides get read and
    the rule either changes or is confirmed. Confirming counts -- a rule
    reaffirmed on evidence is stronger than one nobody has examined.)


⚠️ FOUR ROLES, AT LEAST THREE PEOPLE, ON DESKS YOU ALREADY HAVE. The proposer and the
outcome logger may be the same person. This does not mean new hires.
Name what people already do, then say which of them is allowed to decide.


DECIDE NOW WHAT HAPPENS ON THE FIRST OVERRIDE
================================================================
Write this while nothing is at stake, so that when it happens in front of the
whole team you are reading a line rather than making a ruling:

  An override INSIDE the stated grounds is the reviewer's call. It gets logged
  with its reason and goes to the revision.

  An override OUTSIDE those grounds goes to the exception authority.

Blur those two and either the reviewer starts asking permission for
everything, or every awkward case quietly becomes an "exception" that the
reviewer decides anyway.

There are two easy answers when a senior person overrides in public, and both
are wrong. OVERRULE THEM and the message lands that the rule outranks judgment
-- so within a month people follow a rule they can see is wrong, which is worse
than having none. WAVE IT THROUGH with a shrug and the rule is decorative by
Thursday.

  (Copy this block as often as you need. A rule that records only its failures
   teaches you nothing; one that records only its successes is a marketing

AT THE REVISION
================================================================
Read the quarter's overrides together, not one at a time. What you are looking
for is a SHAPE -- four overrides that share a reason are the rule telling you
what it's blind to.

  Overrides this quarter: ______   Shape they share: ________________

  [ ] The rule CHANGES. New wording:

      ______________________________________________________________

  [ ] The rule is CONFIRMED, on the evidence. (Confirmed twice running? That's
      a flag, not a comfort -- either it's genuinely settled or nobody's
      reading. Ask the proposer, not the reviewer. They'll know.)

  [ ] The RECORD needs a new field, because the rule is blind to something the
      record never captured. (This is the loop closing in the other direction,
      and it's often the most valuable outcome of a revision.)

  Next revision date: __________________________________________


TWO TRAPS
================================================================
THE RULE THAT'S REALLY AN AVERAGE. Write "do whatever usually works" and you
have encoded the middle of your record and thrown away the edges -- and the
edges are frequently where your advantage lives. A good rule handles the common
case and NAMES what it doesn't cover.

MORE THAN ONE RULE. See the top of this page. One. Really.
