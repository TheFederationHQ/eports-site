How to read it Asking prices are hopes; sold prices are facts

Active listings tell you what sellers hope to get. Sold listings tell you what buyers actually paid. The median of a handful of genuinely matching sales — rounded down, so the price you plan on is one you'll probably beat — is the most honest single number a seller can carry into a store.

A worked example

Five matching sold comps: $38, $41, $44, $46 and $58. The median is $44 — the $58 outlier doesn't move it — and rounded down to a clean price it's $40. With only two comps, the scorecard says walk: two sales are a coincidence, not a market. For the demand check, 180 listed against 220 sold in about ninety days moves; 400 listed against 60 sold — nearly seven waiting for every one that sells — is slow.

Common questions

Why round down?

So the plan has room in it. Everything after this — the fee, the shipping, the most you can pay — is built on this number; building on an optimistic one is how a good-looking flip loses money.

Why is the demand check "rough"?

Because search counts are messy and the sold filter only reaches back about ninety days. It's a shape you read in a minute in an aisle, not a rate to two decimal places — and a "maybe" is fine: that's what a short test is for.
