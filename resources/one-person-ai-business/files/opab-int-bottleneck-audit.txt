THE BOTTLENECK AUDIT  —  where your hours actually go
From "The One-Person AI Business — Intermediate", Chapter 1
================================================================

"I'm the bottleneck" is true but useless. A bottleneck has a SHAPE — a
specific place the flow jams — and until you know the shape you'll systematize
the wrong thing first. So you measure. For ONE ordinary week you log what you
do, tagged by function, AS YOU GO — not from memory on Friday, which is where
this goes wrong.

⚠️ This is the most valuable week of measurement you'll do, because every later
chapter is aimed by it. It's mildly annoying for a week. Do it anyway.


THE LOG  (rough blocks, tagged as you go — not reconstructed later)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag every block with ONE function: DEMAND (getting attention) · SALES (turning
attention into buyers) · DELIVERY (making/sending the thing) · SUPPORT (looking
after customers) · FINANCE (the numbers).

THE FOUR THINGS THE LOG TELLS YOU
----------------------------------------------------------------
  1. WHERE THE HOURS ACTUALLY ARE — usually not where you thought.
       Biggest function: ______________________________________

  2. JUDGMENT vs GRIND — grind is repetitive, rule-shaped, low-decision work.
     It hands off first and most safely; judgment you keep longest.
       The grind inside that function: ________________________

  3. FIRST TO DELEGATE — the function that, run without you, frees the most
     hours for the LEAST risk to quality.
       First function to hand off: ____________________________

  4. WHAT "GOOD" LOOKS LIKE — in your own words. You meet this standard
     already; you've just never had to write it down.
       "Good" for that function is: ___________________________
       ________________________________________________________


ASK YOUR REASONING ENGINE  (to see the pattern you're too close to)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  "Here is a week of my hours, tagged by function [paste the log]. Which
   function eats the most time? Within it, which parts are repetitive
   rule-shaped grind versus real judgment? And which single function, handed
   off first, would free the most hours for the least risk to quality?"


THE FORCING COMMITMENT  (write it, dated — this aims the whole book)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  "In the week of ______, my hours split roughly: demand ___, sales ___,
   delivery ___, support ___, finance ___. The function eating the most time is
   ______. The first function I will hand off is ______, because ______."

That last line is a commitment: you've named the first thing to take out of your
own hands, on the evidence, dated — so you can't drift into systematizing
whatever merely feels urgent this week.
