THE ONE-SENTENCE PRODUCT BUILDER
From "Digital Products — Basics", chapter 1
================================================================

Before you make anything, you write one sentence. It forces the four
decisions that a product either gets right or dies on: who it is for, what
problem they have, what it actually is, and what changes for the buyer.

If you cannot fill every blank, you do not yet have a product — you have a
file you hope someone wants. That is the most expensive mistake in this
whole business, and it costs nothing to avoid here.


THE SHAPE
----------------------------------------------------------------

  This is a ____________________ (what it is: a template / printable /
  reference / asset set / micro-course)

  for ____________________________________ (who it is for — be specific;
  "small business owners" is not specific, "freelance designers who invoice
  more than five clients a month" is)

  who ____________________________________ (the problem they have RIGHT NOW,
  in their words, not yours)

  so that ________________________________ (what is different for them after
  — the outcome, never the feature)


WRITE YOURS HERE
----------------------------------------------------------------

  This is a ______________________________________________________

  for ___________________________________________________________

  who ___________________________________________________________

  so that _______________________________________________________


TWO WORKED EXAMPLES
----------------------------------------------------------------

  This is a fill-in spreadsheet
  for freelancers who dread April
  who have a shoebox of receipts and no system
  so that their tax return takes an evening instead of a lost weekend.

  This is a set of 30 editable social templates
  for handmade-jewelry sellers with fewer than 1,000 followers
  who know their photos look amateur next to the big shops
  so that every post looks like it belongs to a real brand.


THE TEST
----------------------------------------------------------------

Read your sentence to someone who is NOT you. If they cannot immediately
say "oh, that's for [type of person]", the "who" is too vague — the single
most common reason a first product never sells. Fix the "who" first.
