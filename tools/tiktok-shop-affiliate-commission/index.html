How to read it The number that survives the return window

When a sale lands, TikTok shows you an estimated commission right away — and it feels like money. It isn't yet. Orders can be returned or refunded for weeks afterward, and when they are, the commission on them is taken back. So the figure worth planning on isn't the estimate — it's the settled commission, what's left once the return window closes.

This tool projects that settled figure from a return rate you enter, and shows it beside the estimate so the gap is impossible to ignore. It's a projection, not your exact payout — real settlement depends on which specific orders come back (and can include partial refunds), and settling is a step before the money reaches your bank. But planned against the settled projection a wave of returns is an annoyance; treated as if the estimate were income, the same wave is a shortfall you already spent.

The formula, in full

Commission per sale = sale price × (rate ÷ 100). Estimated monthly = that per-sale commission × the number of sales you drive. Projected settled monthly = estimated × (1 − return rate ÷ 100) — the share of orders that don't come back. What returns take is simply the difference between the two.

A worked example

A $28 product at a 15% commission pays $4.20 a sale. Drive 40 sales in a month and the app cheerfully shows an estimated $168. But if 12% of those orders are returned, the commission on them gets clawed back — so the projected settled amount is $168 × 0.88 = $147.84, and returns quietly took $20.16. The estimate wasn't fake — it just counted money that returns can still take back before it's yours.

Edge cases the calculator handles

  • A 100% return rate settles to $0 — every order came back, so no commission stays.
  • A 0% return rate makes settled equal estimated. Real products rarely sit there; the point of the box is to make you enter your rate, from your own tracker, not a hopeful guess.

Common questions

What commission rate should I use?

The seller sets it, per product, and it ranges from low single digits up to a large slice — there is no standard. Use the actual rate on the product you're promoting, which you'll see in the affiliate marketplace. Current ranges, with dates, sit on the TikTok Shop Affiliate resources page.

Where do I find my return rate?

From your own settled history, not a number off a hype video. The free settled-commission tracker logs each order as it matures, so your real return rate falls out of your own data.

Is this financial advice or a guarantee?

No. It's arithmetic on numbers you enter. What you actually earn depends on the products, the platform, and buyers — none of which this box controls. It just stops you counting money that hasn't survived the return window yet.
