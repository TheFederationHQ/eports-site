======================================================================
  GOVERN & PROVE THE WHOLE OPERATION
  AI for Your Business — Intermediate, Chapters 9–11
======================================================================

Each function has its own fence. This is the layer ABOVE them — the way
you govern the whole, run it to a weekly rhythm, and prove, on evidence,
that it holds without you.

======================================================================
  1. THE GOVERNANCE ONE-PAGER  (Chapter 9)
======================================================================

THE POLICY THAT BINDS EVERY FUNCTION AT ONCE (a new function inherits
these or it doesn't launch):
  • AI never states a policy, price, or fact that isn't in the brain.
  • AI never moves money or admits fault without a person.
  • Every customer-facing AI is labeled as AI where it matters.
  • Every function keeps its escalation triggers and its fallback.

THE KILL SWITCH (the answer to "who can stop it"):
  For each function — how it's switched off fast (revert to that
  function's manual fallback), and WHO may pull it. Give that person real
  authority: they stop a function the moment it's visibly misbehaving,
  with no meeting and no sign-off, and are never second-guessed for
  stopping it in good faith. (Stopping is reversible. A wave of damage
  isn't.)
    Function ____________  off-switch ____________  holder ____________
    Function ____________  off-switch ____________  holder ____________

A WATCH ON THE WHOLE (not "keep an eye out" — an actual control):
  Governance owner (watches the pattern ACROSS functions): ______________
  One cross-function log every function drops a signal into, on a beat:
    - each function's signal + its MAX interval (customer-facing = daily,
      slower = weekly), so nothing hides for a month;
    - a rough "look now" threshold (spike / run of the same odd output /
      number off its range);
    - the route when one trips: governance owner → the function's
      kill-switch holder → (if customers affected) that function's
      remediation plan.

DATA & LIABILITY:
  A fixed quarterly date to confirm the business-tier settings are still
  right and the approved tools are still the ones being used: __ / __ / __
  You are responsible for what your AI tells your customers, whatever your
  jurisdiction. Put a professional (lawyer / insurer) on the real legal
  questions. Never let AI have the last word on anything you take the blame
  for. (Name the risk; don't take legal advice from a template.)

THE CASCADE DRILL (rehearse it — a policy you haven't tested is fiction).
  Pick a failure that SPREADS: e.g. the service AI, after an update,
  starts telling clients a deadline that's wrong — and the pipeline
  repeats it in quotes, and it lands in account notes as if true. Run the
  clock as a team and answer honestly:
    - Who notices first, and after how long?
    - Through how many functions has it traveled by then?
    - Who has authority to stop ALL of them — do they use it or hesitate?
    - Which customers already have it in writing; who calls them, by when?
  Close every gap it exposes: authority to stop any function on sight, a
  shared place signals land, a "wrong information out" plan.

======================================================================
  2. THE WEEKLY OPERATING CADENCE  (Chapter 10)
======================================================================

One short, standing weekly meeting — same day, same time, 30 minutes,
standing — that reads the operation's instruments and acts. Not a status
meeting; a pulse.

  Fixed slot: ______________  Chair (hand to a senior now and then): ______

  Running order (each owner reports their own — you mostly listen):
    [ ] Each function, its ONE number (gate pass-rate / SLA / recovery /
        critical-miss count / open items on the register). A number off
        its range is the reason to be in the room.
    [ ] The cross-function catch (the pipeline owner says "heavy week
        coming"; service staffs up BEFORE the queue backs up).
    [ ] Brain & rules kept current (a new "never", a spec change, a fix
        from an exception log — ratified and folded into the brain).
    [ ] The register, chased (every open decision: done / on track / stuck;
        stuck ones get an owner + date on the spot).

  THE RULE that keeps it from decaying into a status meeting: nothing
  that's off leaves the room without a DISPOSITION — a named owner, one
  action, a date — on the same register, chased next week until closed.

======================================================================
  3. THE OWNER-ABSENT VERIFICATION  (Chapter 11)  — the proof
======================================================================

The only honest test of "it runs without me" is to leave — and to check
the EVIDENCE, not your gut, when you're back. Reassurance ("all good?")
isn't proof; a trail is.

STEP 1 — WRITE THE EXAM BEFORE YOU GO (so you can't grade to what you
find):
  Set a bar PER FUNCTION — they differ — not one bar for all (at least 3):

   Function             Sample size   Pass bar (its standard)   A critical miss is
   _______________      _________     ____________________      _________________
   _______________      _________     ____________________      _________________
   _______________      _________     ____________________      _________________

  THE RULE (every function): one with a critical miss, or a control left
  uncovered while you were away, does NOT pass — it gets fixed and re-run.

STEP 2 — GO GENUINELY DARK for a set window (a week if you can, a few days
minimum). No cc, no check-in. Tell the team it's a proof, not a trap, and
to keep the trail.

STEP 3 — AUDIT ON THE EVIDENCE when you're back (five checks):
  [ ] Sampled output vs the spec — pull the sample you pre-set; does it
      pass the written test?
  [ ] Usage, not lip service — did they run the loops, or quietly revert?
      (brain markers / gate records / tool usage)
  [ ] Gate compliance — what got HELD: escalations fired, drafts sent
      back, refusals routed to a person.
  [ ] The exception & register trail — oddities caught, cross-function log
      flagged, the cadence chased in your absence.
  [ ] The numbers — each function's measure vs the range you'd marked
      normal.

STEP 4 — ADD THE TEAM'S ACCOUNT as ONE input, not the verdict. Where it
disagrees with the trail, the trail wins.

STEP 5 — READ IT STRAIGHT. Functions clearing the bar you set = your
proof; bank it. A function that drifted or had a control off = a
diagnosis, not a defeat: name it, fix the gap, and re-run that one.
