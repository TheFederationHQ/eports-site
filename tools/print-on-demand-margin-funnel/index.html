A bigger average basket lifts margin, not just revenue — because one order pays the per-order fee once. That is what the repeat and cross-sell engine is really buying you. A blanket coupon, though, can gut a thin design. Ten percent off a $16 item knocks $1.60 off the price — but you keep ~13% of that in the fee you no longer pay, so a $1.57 contribution falls to about 18¢: not below zero, but almost nothing left, and a deeper coupon does turn negative. So set a minimum-basket floor. (The coupon check uses the platform fee % from the margin box above.)

How to read it Two instruments, read separately

Contribution is what one sale leaves you after base cost, shipping, the platform's percentage and its fixed fee. Total contribution is that figure times how many sold in a window — the number that actually pays your bills, and the one you judge a design on.

The funnel tells you where a design fails, and each pattern points somewhere different to look first: few views is a discovery problem (get it seen), plenty of views but almost no favorites is a listing problem (photo, title, offer, price), and favorites with no orders is likely friction at checkout (price, shipping, thin reviews). None of these is a proven verdict — they are the first place to look.

Keep a copy: the same dashboard as a spreadsheet, and the fill-in cull worksheet that turns these numbers into a keep-or-cut decision.
