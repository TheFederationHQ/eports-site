01 — What changed, and when The ten things most guides still get wrong

These are the claims from appendix B of the book. Each one carries the date it changed and the date we last confirmed it. A heavier left bar means the rule changed recently enough that most advice online has not caught up.

  • Etsy “made by seller” now means made from scratch — Canva templates are excluded

    Changed June 2025  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    The Creativity Standards require that what you sell was made, designed, handpicked or sourced by you. Reselling a bought template, or a light edit of one, is a policy violation rather than a grey area. This is the single change that invalidates the most digital-product advice still circulating, because “make it once, sell it forever” was the whole pitch.

    Check it: Etsy Seller Policy — House Rules (see the Creativity Standards section)

  • Etsy fees — 6.5% transaction fee, charged on the shipping price too

    Still current  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    Plus a listing fee per item and a separate payment-processing fee on top. The part people miss is that the 6.5% is applied to the total including what you charge for postage, so free shipping is not free.

    Check it: Etsy Fees & Payments Policy

  • Amazon Associates reportedly cut commissions by up to half, and narrowed its onsite commission

    Changed spring 2026 (agreement 14 April)  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    Two changes in the same direction. The reported rate cut (from around March 2026, never published by Amazon) is the big one. The 14 April 2026 agreement also narrowed commission on Amazon’s own onsite placements to the exact product linked; ordinary links you send still earn on other items bought in the same visit. Any income figure published before 2026 should be treated as fiction.

    Check it: Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement — and the current fee schedule linked from it

  • Amazon Associates stopped paying recurring commission on subscriptions

    Changed January 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    The “build passive recurring income” model that a great deal of affiliate content was built to sell no longer exists in that form on Amazon. One payment, not a stream.

    Check it: the fee schedule inside the Associates Program Operating Agreement

  • Google shows an AI summary for about a fifth of search terms, and most searches now end without a click

    Still current in 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    SparkToro’s analysis of Similarweb data found 68% of US Google searches ended with no click to any website (January–April 2026); separate Ahrefs research found AI Overviews on 21% of the keywords it tracked. ⚠️ These come from independent search-industry measurement, not from Google, so treat the exact numbers as a range rather than a fact. The direction is not in dispute: ranking for a question no longer means being visited.

    Check it: there is no official page for this one. Look for measurement published in the last six months and check who funded it — question 1 applies to this claim as much as to any other.

  • YouTube does not monetise mass-produced or templated content — but faceless channels are fine

    Still current, clarified July 2025  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    This is the most widely misreported rule in the book. The policy targets mass production: templated scripts with words swapped out, slideshows with little or no real narration. A faceless channel with original scripting and real narration is fully monetisable. If someone told you faceless was banned, they were reading headlines rather than the policy.

    Check it: YouTube monetisation policies

  • TikTok Shop takes 6% in the US (its published table; many sellers report 8% since August 2026) and 9% in the UK, with a reduced rate for new sellers

    Still current in 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    The US referral fee covers both commission and payment processing in one number. The new-seller rate of 3% runs for 30 days — note that it is conditional, not automatic: you have to make a first sale within a set window of onboarding to trigger it. Select jewellery categories sit lower. Refunds carry a separate administration fee.

    Check it: TikTok Shop Seller Center — confirm your own market and category rather than a blog’s summary

  • TikTok Shop announced a shipping mandate, then reversed it

    Reversed January–February 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    Worth keeping on the list precisely because it was undone. A great deal of content was published during the four weeks it stood and never corrected afterwards. When a platform announces something, the useful habit is to check whether it actually took effect before you rebuild anything around it.

    Check it: TikTok Shop Seller Center announcements

  • Shopify renamed its middle plan from “Shopify” to “Grow”

    Still current in 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    A small thing with an outsized effect on searching: any tutorial telling you to pick “the Shopify plan” is now ambiguous, because that is also the brand name. Compare the transaction-fee rows rather than the plan names.

    Check it: Shopify pricing

  • Pinterest: pin steadily rather than in bursts, and avoid link shorteners

    Still current in 2026  ·  Last checked 12 October 2026

    Commonly quoted as 5–15 pins a day. ⚠️ That number is community guidance, not a published Pinterest rule — the published rule is the spam policy, which flags deceptive or obscured destinations. Shorteners hide the destination, which is why they attract trouble. Pinterest matters disproportionately here because it is the one major platform that will still send someone to your shop in a single tap without you paying for it.

    Check it: Pinterest community guidelines (see the Spam section)

⚠️ Every row above will eventually be wrong. That is not a defect in the list, it is the reason the list has dates on it. If a date here is more than three months old, treat the row as unverified and check the platform’s page yourself — which is exactly what the three questions below are for.

02 — The habit The three questions

These work on any business advice, including this book and including this page. They are the part with no expiry date, which is why they appear in every book in the series rather than only in the first one. Print this and keep it somewhere you will see it.

  1. Who gets paid if I follow this and it does not work? Not who is right — who is paid. It is a faster question and it has a checkable answer.
  2. What is the date on this specific claim? Not the date on the video. The date on the claim inside it. A 2026 upload repeating a 2022 fee is a 2022 claim.
  3. Can I reach the platform’s own page in two clicks? If nobody will show you where the number comes from, that is the answer.

If a claim fails all three, it is not necessarily wrong — but it is unverified, and it should be treated as a rumour until you have checked it yourself.

04 — One page Start from your constraint, not from the model

There is no best model. Each of the five fails for a specific, predictable reason, and each of those reasons maps onto something you either have or do not have. So the useful question is not which model is best — it is which constraint is yours.

  • Money is shortYou have time, but little you can afford to lose
    Print on demand, or digital products Both let you spend hours instead of pounds. Do not start with dropshipping, whatever the marketing says — it looks free and it is the model that most needs an advertising budget. Pair either with Pinterest.
  • Time is shortYou have some money, but a job and a life
    A marketplace The calculation inverts. Your scarce resource is evenings, and free traffic costs a great many of them. A marketplace does the finding and charges a percentage, which is the correct trade when your hours are worth more than 6.5%.
  • You have never done itAnd you are not sure you can
    Whatever can be live this week Pick the shortest distance between doing something and finding out whether it worked — a marketplace listing, or a one-page digital product. The point of the first one is not income. It is converting “I think I could” into “I have.”
  • You already have peopleEven a small audience, even from something unrelated
    Ask them what they would buy Start there and ignore most of the above. Everything hard is about getting strangers to arrive. If people already listen to you, you are starting from the far end.

The one that applies to everybody

Collect email addresses from the first day. Every model sits on a platform that can change the deal without telling you — two of the rows in section 01 are exactly that happening. A list is the only thing you build that nobody else can switch off, and the cost of starting it on day one instead of month six is nothing.

Telling the difference between slow and wrong

At about the six-week mark most people have the same private thought: is this not working, or am I not being patient? Slow looks like small numbers moving in the right direction — a few visitors, then a few more, an occasional sale, one person who came back. Wrong looks like nothing at all: no traffic, or traffic that never buys, and no change over weeks of effort. Slow means continue. Wrong means change one thing — the product, the audience, or the traffic source — and give it another honest run. It does not mean switching models, because that restarts the same clock from zero having learned nothing you can carry over.

05 — Check it yourself Every platform’s own page, in one place

This is question 3 made practical. Fees and policies live on pages the platform controls, and those pages are the only version that counts. Bookmark the ones for the model you picked and read them before you read anybody’s summary of them — including ours.

  • Etsy Fees & Payments Policy The authoritative fee list, including what the percentage is actually charged on.
  • Etsy Seller Policy — House Rules Contains the Creativity Standards, which is the section that decides whether what you want to sell is allowed at all.
  • Shopify pricing Compare the transaction-fee rows, not the plan names — the names have changed and will change again.
  • TikTok Shop Seller Center Rates differ by market and category. Confirm yours here rather than trusting a figure from a video.
  • Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement Includes the required disclosure wording and links to the current fee schedule. Both have changed recently.
  • YouTube monetisation policies Read the actual wording on mass-produced content before believing anything about faceless channels.
  • Pinterest community guidelines The Spam section is the one that governs how you may link out.
  • Amazon KDP Hyperlink Guidelines Relevant if you publish anything on Kindle yourself. It is also the rule that shapes this page: a book may link to a resource page, but never to a bare email-capture form.

If one of these links has gone dead, the page moved rather than the policy disappearing. Search the platform’s own help centre for the page name given above, and tell us so we can fix the row.
