======================================================================
  THE FUNCTION DEPLOYMENT KIT
  AI for Your Business — Intermediate, Chapters 3–7
======================================================================

One repeatable template for standing up ANY function on AI so it runs to
standard, operated by your team, without you in the loop. Copy it once
per function. Parts A and D apply to every function; use Part B for
customer-facing functions and Part C for back-office ones.

======================================================================
  PART A — THE LOOP & THE GATE  (every function)
======================================================================

THE LOOP (the work runs the same way every time; none of the steps is you):
  1. Brain in   — operator starts from the operating brain (the canonical
                  project), never a blank chat, and adds the specifics.
  2. Draft out  — the AI produces a first version, shaped by the brain.
  3. Check      — the work is checked against a written standard — but HOW
                  it's checked depends on the function (see the release model).
  4. Release    — it goes out. The constant is that the standard is written
                  and the check is never you. The MODEL varies:

THE RELEASE MODEL — pick the one that fits this function. They are NOT all
per-item sign-off; forcing that on every function just rebuilds the
bottleneck you're removing.
  ( ) Per-item approval — a named approver signs every piece before it goes.
      For high-stakes, lower-volume work (client comms). Uses the gate below.
  ( ) Escalation-only — the AI runs automatically; a person only touches the
      calls on the never-decide line (a sales pipeline: capture, qualify and
      draft run on their own; price / promise / refusal escalate). See Part B.
  ( ) Sampling — the work releases, and a named checker marks a SAMPLE
      against the definition of done, by the sampling rule. For high-volume
      back-office work. See Part C.

THE PUBLISH GATE (for the per-item model — your standard, made portable, a
fixed list, not a feeling). Fill in 5–6 yes/no questions every piece must
pass. Anything short of a clear YES goes back to step 2; nothing that hasn't
cleared them all reaches a customer.

  PART B — CUSTOMER-FACING EXTRAS  (sales pipeline, service)

THE NEVER-DECIDE LINE (things AI drafts but never DECIDES). At least:
  • PRICE   — AI lays out standard rates; a person sets the number sent.
  • PROMISE — any deadline / guarantee / "yes we can" is a person's word.
  • REFUSAL — who you turn away is a person's judgment.
  Add any other call where a wrong AI answer costs real money:
  • ____________________________________________________________________

THE ESCALATION RULE + SLA (for a pipeline):
  Trigger (price asked / promise wanted / refuse-flag / AI unsure) →
  routes to (name): __________________  backup: __________________
  Response promise: an escalated item gets a real answer within ____ hours.
  A breach doesn't just remind — it MOVES the work: past the clock the
  backup becomes owner; past ____ hours it goes to __________________ and
  the AI sends nothing new until a person has it.

AUTHORITY CAP (for customer service — what AI may NEVER do):
  • Never move money (no refund / credit / discount — a person decides).
  • Never admit fault or liability (sorry for the EXPERIENCE, never the
    cause: "we were negligent" / "that was our error" are evidence).
  • Never invent policy ("let me check and come back" is always safe).
  Pre-approved menu it MAY offer (specific gestures only): ______________

SEVERITY & HANDOFF (route by how bad it is; a handoff is a transfer of
ownership, not a notification — named owner + backup + deadline + the
whole thread attached):
  Routine (question / mild grumble)  → stays with AI (spot-checked)
  Remedy ask (wants money / a fix)   → ____________  by: same business day
  Hot (angry / complaint / repeat / "get me a human") → ______ within 1 hr
  Serious (threat / safety / injury / vulnerable) → ______ immediately

MEASURE RECOVERY, NOT DEFLECTION: of customers who arrived unhappy, how
many stayed / were sorted without a repeat / confirmed it was resolved;
plus correct-handoff rate and (the one that matters) MISSED-handoff rate.
Keep deflection small, at the bottom.

======================================================================
  PART D — MAKING IT STICK: THE CONDITIONAL SUNSET  (Chapter 4)
======================================================================

The new way only sticks when the old way stops being an option — but you
force adoption WITHOUT burning the house down. Set a date, and four
conditions that must be true by then:

  Target date to switch the old way OFF (2–4 weeks out): __ / __ / ____

  1. PROOF IT HOLDS — two clean weeks on the measured test (nothing walked
     back, every piece through the route, no unplanned owner rescues).
  2. PROOF IT'S BUILT — operator trained · approver cover named · a
     one-page "here's the loop" for new hires · escalation line tested
     on a real awkward case.
  3. A FALLBACK for when the tool is down — a narrow emergency path that
     survives the tool itself being unavailable (kept OUTSIDE the tool).
  4. A NAMED ROLLBACK TRIGGER — the one condition that says "stop, go
     back": _______________________________________________________

  On the date: conditions met → archive the old way (don't merely
  discourage it). Not met → the sunset doesn't happen; book a named review
  date to try again. Proof gates it; the calendar can't force it.
