Before you hire

Four checks that take ten minutes

  1. LicenseLook it up on your state's official site below. Make sure it's active and the name matches the company on your quote.
  2. InsuranceAsk for a certificate of liability insurance, and workers' compensation if they have a crew. Call the insurer if in doubt.
  3. PermitsAsk who pulls the permit. Work done without one can fail inspection or cause trouble when you sell.
  4. Contract and paymentsGet the price, scope and schedule in writing. Pay in stages as work is done, not most of it up front.
FAQ

Contractor licenses, answered

How do I check if a contractor is licensed?

Find your state below and open its official lookup. Search by the contractor's name or license number, then check that the license is active, matches the company on your quote, and covers the type of work you're hiring for.

What if my state has no state contractor license?

Several states leave general contractors to cities and counties. Call your city or county building department, ask the contractor for any local license or registration, and check trade licenses (electrical, plumbing) separately.

What else should I check besides the license?

Ask for proof of liability insurance and, if they have staff, workers' compensation. Get a written contract with the price, scope and schedule, and pull the required permits. Be wary of large cash deposits up front.

Is an online license lookup always up to date?

It's the official record, but there can be a short delay after a change. If something looks wrong, call the licensing agency before you sign.
