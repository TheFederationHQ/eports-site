$3.71 in, $28 out: checking the '80% margin' in this week's viral AI dropshipping tutorial

A new tutorial from one of YouTube's biggest ecommerce channels shows dog earmuffs bought for $3.71 and sold for about $28. The 80% is real on the product alone. Here's what's left after a real month of tools, fees, shipping and refunds, and the one cost US sellers can't skip any more.

Edited by Drew Halden12 Nov 2026 Sources checked 12 Nov 2026

ShareXFacebookLinkedIn

The short version: A new dropshipping tutorial from Wholesale Ted, one of YouTube's biggest ecommerce channels, shows a store selling dog earmuffs for about $28 that it can buy for $3.71. She calls it "a profit margin of over 80%." On the product alone, that's true.

Once you add a month of tools, card fees, shipping, duty and refunds, our worked example keeps about 14% at 10 orders a month and about 55% at 100.

That's still a good business at volume. But the first month is where most new stores live, and that's where the 80% gets thin.

Who can ignore this: sellers who already run a store and know their cost per order. If you're about to start one because of a video like this, read on.

What the video actually shows

The tutorial is a clear, well-made walk-through. You build a Shopify store, add products with a dropshipping app, then make AI videos to get free views on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. Her example is a reel of a scared puppy calmed by a pair of earmuffs, which she says passed 9 million views in about 5 weeks.

The store in the reel charges about NZ$50, which she converts to about US$28. She found the same earmuffs on AutoDS for $3.71. "You do the math," she says. "That is a profit margin of over 80%."

So we did. $28 minus $3.71 leaves $24.29, which is 86.75% of the price. That's the gap between what the buyer pays and what the supplier charges. It isn't what lands in your bank account.

The video is sponsored by AutoDS, and the store builder she uses carries her affiliate link. She says so on screen, which is the right way to do it.

The order has more mouths to feed

Every sale pays for four things before any of it is yours: the product, the shipping, the payment fee and any refunds. US sellers now add a fifth.

Card fees. Shopify Payments on the Basic plan charges 2.9% plus 30 cents per online card sale. On $28 that's about $1.11.

Shipping. Our example assumes $3.50 a parcel from the supplier to a US address. Your real figure depends on the supplier and the weight, so get it in writing before you list.

Refunds. A cheap item that ships from abroad will come back sometimes. We set aside 5% of the price, about $1.40 an order. Change it once you know your own rate.

With duty (below), that's about $11.22 of costs on each $28 order. You keep about $16.78, or 60%, before you've paid for the store itself.

The cost the tutorial skips: duty on every parcel

She films from New Zealand, and her example store sells in New Zealand dollars. US sellers face a rule she doesn't cover.

The US used to let parcels worth under $800 in without duty. That exemption is now suspended indefinitely for every country, by mail and by courier, so each order your supplier ships from abroad pays duty. The rate depends on the product and where it's made.

Our example puts duty and handling at $1.50 an order, as a placeholder. Ask your supplier for the landed price to a US address, duty included, before you set your own price.

Then the monthly bills arrive

Shopify's current offer is 3 days free, then $1 a month for 3 months. After that, Basic is $39 a month.

AutoDS starts at $26.90 a month, but that plan doesn't watch supplier prices or stock. To get what the video describes, you'd need Starter at $39.90 plus the $9.90 order add-on that places orders for you. The AI video tool she uses, CreateUGC, is listed as a $39.90 add-on.

That's about $128.70 a month before you sell anything. Here's what's left once the promos end:

Our example breaks even at about 8 orders a month. Below that, the store costs you money. At zero orders it costs $128.70.

The table leaves out your own time, and making a good showcase video takes hours, not minutes. It also assumes free views. If a video doesn't take off and you switch to paid ads, the ad cost per order comes straight out of that $16.78.

What to do before you build the store

  1. Run your own numbers in our free dropshipping profit margin calculator. Put in the landed cost, your real shipping and an ad cost, even if you hope it's zero.
  2. Get the landed price in writing, to a US address with duty included, from at least two suppliers.
  3. Work out your break-even. Divide your monthly tools bill by what you keep per order. That's the number of orders you need each month just to stand still.
  4. Cancel what you don't use before trials end. Several of these tools start billing the day the trial runs out.
  5. Keep your videos honest. An AI "customer" saying "my kids love it" is a made-up testimonial, and the FTC's rule bans those, AI-made or not. A labelled demo of the product working is fine.

Costing one sale this way, landed price, shipping, fees and a refund allowance, is the first money chapter of Dropshipping Basics, and the costing sheet is free in our dropshipping resources.

What we're watching

The earmuff trend is seasonal, built around fireworks nights, so we'll see whether the stores selling them keep going after November. We'll also update this story if US duty rules change again.
