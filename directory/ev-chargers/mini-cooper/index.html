Before you buy

Getting it installed, in four steps

  1. Check your panelAsk an electrician whether your panel has room for a 40, 50 or 60-amp circuit. That decides your charger, not the other way round.
  2. Pick plug-in or hardwiredPlug-in units use a NEMA 14-50 outlet and top out at 40 A. 48 A needs hardwiring, which also avoids cheap outlets overheating.
  3. Get the permitMost towns want a permit for a new 240 V circuit. Your electrician usually files it.
  4. Ask about rebatesMany power companies pay toward a home charger or give a cheaper night rate. Check your utility's website before you buy.
FAQ

Questions MINI Cooper owners ask

How long does a MINI Cooper take to charge at home?

About 4 hours from empty to full on a 240-volt Level 2 charger, going by the EPA's test of the 2024 Cooper SE Hardtop 2 door. On a normal 120-volt outlet it would take roughly 25 hours. Most nights you only top up what you drove that day.

What size charger do I need for a MINI Cooper?

A 40-amp charger covers the speed this car charged at in the EPA test, and runs on a 50-amp circuit. If you might change cars in the next few years, a 48-amp charger costs about the same and covers almost any EV.

Can I charge a MINI Cooper from a normal wall outlet?

Yes, with the cable that comes with the car, at about 5 miles of range per hour. That's enough if you drive under about 30 to 40 miles a day.

Which plug does the MINI Cooper use?

J1772, or NACS on some newer models. Check your port before you buy: a round five-pin port is J1772; a small Tesla-style port is NACS. Buy the charger with the matching cable, or use a safety-listed adapter.

How much does it cost to charge a MINI Cooper at home?

A full charge takes about 35 kWh from the wall, about $6 at 17 cents a kWh. Driving 40 miles a day costs roughly $63 a month. Use the calculator on this page with your own electricity price.
