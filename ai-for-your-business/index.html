Book 1 · BasicsFind where AI pays across your operation, and bank a verified recurring saving

For: you run a business with staff, you're AI-curious, and you half-suspect it's real for other firms but you're not sure it's real for yours — and you're wary of wasting money, looking foolish, breaking something, or unsettling your team. Book 2 · IntermediateRun whole functions on AI — operated by your team, governed, and proven to run without you

For: AI already pays on a task or two, but it's ad-hoc and one person drives it. You want it across the team, sticking, and governed — without it turning into a mess. You finish with: at least three core functions running on AI to a documented standard, operated by your team, and proven — on evidence gathered while you were genuinely out of the room, not a hopeful "all good" — to run without you. Comes with, free: the rollout plan, the operating-brain template, the function deployment kit, the monthly management pack and decision register, and the govern & prove kit — on the worksheets page.

Book 3 · AdvancedTurn AI into an advantage rivals can't easily copy

For: your operation runs on AI and it's plateaued as an advantage — because the same tools are for sale to everyone, so efficiency alone no longer sets you apart. You finish with: one AI advantage deployed that is materially harder to copy — it depends on an asset you own — beating a baseline you registered before you could see the answer, running without you or the person who owns it, under a contracted commitment to a paying client, and past a completed dependency and durability audit — or a precise diagnosis of which edge failed to materialise, and the constraint behind it.

Eleven chapters. It opens by taking something away: the evidence says a firm your size will not build a data moat, so chapter one is a selection gate that eliminates rather than a list that inspires — four candidate edges in, one out, with a measured baseline and a dated go/no-go. Then five layers built on that one choice: the record of what worked rather than what happened; one recurring decision bound to a rule that sharpens every quarter; a priced pilot to a single real client; a controlled test of what it's actually worth, measured in margin per delivery hour; and the reliability standard clients reorganise their own week around. Then governed autonomy at scale, the reckoning — a durability audit of how much of this is really yours — the decision it forces, the deploy of one owned-asset advantage under a contracted commitment to a named client, and the quarterly review that keeps the edge compounding.

Comes with, free: five worksheets, published free alongside the book — the edge selection gate, the governed outcome record, the decision-rule card, the pilot-and-pricing test, and the durability audit with its quarterly review.
