THE STAIRCASE RANGE PLANNER
From "Digital Products — Intermediate", chapter 3
================================================================

A range is not more products. It is the SAME buyer, offered the next step up,
built mostly from work you have already done. Three rungs off one piece of
work: an entry, a core, and a premium. The middle one is what most people
should buy — so it is the one you describe in the most detail.

Fill this in for one buyer. If two rungs serve different buyers, you have two
ranges, not one — pick one buyer and start again.

RUNG 2 · CORE   ★ what most people should buy
----------------------------------------------------------------
  What it is:        ____________________________
  Built from Rung 1 PLUS: _______________________ (the added work)
  Price:             £______
  Its job:           the real outcome; the rung you describe in most detail.


RUNG 3 · PREMIUM   (catches the few who always take the top)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  What it is:        ____________________________
  Built from Rung 2 PLUS: _______________________ (usually something that does
                                                   NOT scale — your time, a look
                                                   at their actual situation)
  Price:             £______
  Its job:           priced so you are content whether it sells or not.


⚠️ THE TWO TRAPS
----------------------------------------------------------------
  * Near-identical prices. If Rung 1 is £19 and Rung 2 is £24, nothing on the
    page says what the extra five pounds buys — and the cheap one wins. Make
    the gap mean something.
  * Collapsing the order bump into the ladder. An order bump (a small add-on at
    checkout) and a tier do different jobs. Keep the bump where it is.
