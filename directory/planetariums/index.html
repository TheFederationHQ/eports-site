Stargazing at home

Keep looking up after the show

A first telescope

A small refractor or tabletop reflector shows the Moon's craters, Saturn's rings and Jupiter's moons. Pick one with a steady mount: a wobbly tripod ruins more first nights than a small lens.

See beginner telescopes

A star projector

A home planetarium projector puts stars and constellations on a bedroom ceiling. Look for one that shows real star positions rather than random dots.

FAQ

Planetariums, answered

How do I find a planetarium near me?

Press “Near me” on this page and allow location. The list re-sorts by distance in your browser, and your location never leaves your device. You can also pick your state.

Are planetarium shows open to the public?

Science centers and museums run public shows most weeks. Many college and school planetariums only open to the public on set evenings, so check the website or call before you go.

How much does a planetarium show cost?

It varies widely. Some college and school shows are free, while big science centers charge a separate show ticket on top of entry. Check the venue's own site for current prices.

What's the difference between a planetarium and an observatory?

A planetarium projects the night sky onto a dome indoors, so it works in any weather and in daytime. An observatory has telescopes for looking at the real sky, usually on clear evenings. A few places on this list have both.
